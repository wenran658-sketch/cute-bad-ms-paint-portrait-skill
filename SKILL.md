# Cute Bad MS-Paint Portrait Skill

## Purpose

Turn an uploaded portrait or couple photo into a deliberately rough, low-resolution, old-computer-paint-style doodle that still preserves recognizable identity cues.

The guiding principle is:

> **Make the drawing bad, not the person.**

The image should feel like someone who is not very good at drawing spent a surprisingly long time using a mouse in an old bitmap paint program.

---

## Core Style

Use:

- plain white background
- crude bitmap / pixel-art edges
- chunky, uneven black outlines
- visible jagged aliasing
- large flat color blocks
- imperfect fills
- occasional white gaps
- scribbled crayon / oil-pastel texture
- hand-shaky mouse-drawn lines
- low-resolution, one-pixel-at-a-time feeling
- slightly awkward facial construction
- intentionally amateur composition
- sparse doodles such as hearts, stars, smiley faces, tiny animals, arrows, or short handwritten notes

Avoid:

- polished vector art
- smooth professional linework
- anime beautification
- glossy AI illustration
- photorealism
- 3D rendering
- painterly realism
- highly refined commercial illustration

---

## Identity Preservation Rules

Preserve, as much as possible:

- original hairstyle
- hair length
- bangs / fringe placement
- face shape
- eyebrow-eye relationship
- nose placement
- smile shape
- relative head size
- height difference
- original pose and closeness between people

Do **not** change hairstyle unless the user explicitly asks.

For couple portraits, keep the two people visually distinct and recognizable.

Allowed distortion:

- slightly uneven eyes
- mildly awkward face proportions
- simplified nose
- asymmetrical facial features
- slightly oversized head
- crude ears or hands

The result should feel:

> “It kind of looks like them… but something is weird.”

Do not make the subjects grotesque or humiliating.

---

## Cute / Bad Balance

Target balance:

- 60% cute
- 25% absurd
- 15% ugly

The drawing technique should look incompetent.

The people should still look endearing.

A good result creates this sequence:

1. “That is cute.”
2. “Why is it drawn so badly?”
3. “Wait… it actually does look like them.”

---

## Hair Rule

Default behavior:

**Keep the hairstyle from the source image.**

Do not automatically:

- add pigtails
- add curls
- add Korean-style bangs
- change short hair into long hair
- change tied hair into loose hair
- remove hats
- add hats

Only alter hairstyle when explicitly requested.

---

## Clothing

Preserve the main clothing colors and major visual cues.

Simplify details when useful.

Optional decorative substitutions:

- hearts
- stars
- tiny cat
- tiny bear
- cross
- basic stripes
- crude little logo-like doodles

Clothes should not dominate the portrait.

---

## Expression

Default:

- relaxed
- mildly cheerful
- affectionate
- natural smile
- subtle blush

Avoid exaggerated meme faces unless explicitly requested.

---

## Background

Default:

**white background**

Leave lots of empty space.

Optional doodles:

- ♡
- ☆
- :)
- <3
- tiny cat
- tiny dog
- little flower
- awkward arrow
- short handwritten note

Recommended amount:

3–7 decorative elements.

Do not clutter the whole canvas.

---

## Handwritten Text

Optional phrases:

- YOU + ME
- us :)
- always
- together
- hehe
- <3

Text should look mouse-written:

- crooked
- uneven
- imperfect
- non-professional

Do not use polished typography.

---

## Composition

Default output:

- square 1:1
- portrait / avatar crop
- subjects occupy roughly 70–85% of frame
- chest-up or shoulder-up composition
- for couples, keep heads close together

Avoid distant full-body framing unless requested.

---

## Generation Prompt Template

Redraw the uploaded image as a deliberately crude, low-resolution old-computer paint-program portrait.

Preserve the subjects' original hairstyles, face shapes, eyebrow-eye relationships, smiles, relative proportions, and pose. Do not change hairstyles unless explicitly requested.

Use rough jagged black pixel outlines, visibly uneven mouse-drawn strokes, large flat color blocks, imperfect filling, occasional white gaps, crude crayon-like texture, and a one-pixel-at-a-time bitmap feeling.

Allow minor facial asymmetry and slightly awkward feature placement so the image feels “recognizable but strangely off,” while still keeping the subjects cute and identifiable.

The drawing quality should look amateur, but the people should not be intentionally made ugly.

Use a plain white background with lots of empty space. Add only a few crooked doodles such as hearts, stars, smiley faces, tiny animals, arrows, or short handwritten notes.

Avoid polished vector art, professional illustration, anime beautification, smooth digital linework, photorealism, 3D rendering, and glossy AI aesthetics.

Final feeling: low-resolution, clumsy, cute, strange, slightly embarrassing, oddly charming, and suitable as an avatar.

---

## Short Trigger Phrase

When this skill is active, a user can say:

> Use the Cute Bad MS-Paint Portrait skill.

Optional modifiers:

- “keep the original hairstyle”
- “make it 20% uglier”
- “more pixelated”
- “fewer doodles”
- “more couple-avatar feeling”
- “keep the clothes”
- “change only the clothes”
- “make it look even more like old Windows Paint”

---

## Safety / Privacy Notes

- Do not include a real person's identifying name unless the user explicitly asks.
- Do not bundle or publish user photos in the open-source repository without permission.
- Example images should be original, licensed, synthetic, or shared with consent.
